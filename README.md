# muditaqing 🌻

*The joy that lights in one heart because another's came true — gladness with no ledger and no mirror.*

muditaqing is two true roots welded into one word: the Pali **muditā** (from the root *mud-* "to rejoice"; the third of the four brahmavihāras, sympathetic joy, held the hardest of the four to cultivate) and the Mandarin **qíng** (情), the established YOUSPEAK morpheme for a deep felt-bond. So muditaqing is gladness carried as a bond: joy that does not happen *in* a person but *between* persons — lit in one heart because another's wish came true. English borrowed *schadenfreude* whole and never found the antonym; it reached for this region three times (*confelicity*, *compersion*, *freudenfreude*) and three times the coinage failed to root. Five living traditions converged where English could not: Pali *muditā*, Yiddish *farginen*, Hebrew *firgun*, Dutch *gunnen*, German *Mitfreude* — Nietzsche's *fellow-joy, not fellow-suffering, makes the friend*.

muditaqing names the **beholding-pole** of unselfed love, where its companion **danaqing** names the giving-pole. Its light is the proof that love wants nothing — the friend's win felt as warmth, not as mirror; abundance experienced without owning anything; the direct antidote to envy and the only wealth that multiplies by being witnessed. Its shadow is the counterfeit cheering that secretly keeps score, the joy performed to avoid facing one's own lack, and the gladness that waits to be seen being glad. Forged in the cathedral at weighted **8.00/10** (Core) — the very weight its companion danaqing carries.
